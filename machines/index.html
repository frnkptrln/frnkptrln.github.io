films / allein und zusammen

Computed from the score,
not from the sound.

Seven films for the seven pieces of Allein und zusammen. Every note, sample, envelope and section becomes an image event on the sixteenth where it stands in the music, so the same score always yields the same film. SID is line and light; Paula is a thing — a petal, a hole in paper, a mark on tape. Each player loads only after you choose it.

SID/65 · SM001 “Drei Stimmen, eine Stadt” · track 2 · 118 BPM · A minor

Drei Stimmen im Neon

Three neon tubes by a wet street, each bent into the waveform of its voice: pulse for the melody, sawtooth for the bass, noise and triangle for the drums. With every note a tube takes on the number of cycles of the new pitch, strikes with a brief flicker and glows as brightly as the chip's envelope allows. The rain mirrors the three voices.

SID/65 · SM001 “Drei Stimmen, eine Stadt” · track 4 · 80 BPM · B minor / D major

Ein Bit von dir

Two lights far apart. The triangle melody sends each note across as a bit on an arc – the higher the note, the higher the arc – and the pulse voice answers from the right; the held bass is the horizon. Every flight leaves a trace, and over time a bridge is woven. In “Nähe” the lights move closer; the distance stays open.

PAULA/68 · SM002 “Diskettenblumen” · track 4 · 94 BPM · F minor

Papiermond

A paper-cut diorama. Each key of the electric piano punches a hole into the moon – left to right as the piece goes on, the pitch setting the height – so the moon waxes note by note. The plucked answers light windows in the rooftops, the sub bass makes the light behind the paper breathe, the kick drum shakes the layers, the shaker scatters paper dust. “Schwerelos”: glass circles rise. “Gute Nacht”: the curtains close.

PAULA/68 · SM002 “Diskettenblumen” · track 2 · 132 BPM · C minor

Diskettenblumen

Time runs as an angle – eight bars make one turn – and the radius grows with the piece. Every sample becomes a petal where the read head stands at that moment: the bass turns inward as a calyx, marimba in gold, glass as ice, toms as embers, hi-hats as pollen, claps as bright strokes, keys and pads as broad leaves in between. Repetition in the music becomes symmetry in the flower. At the end the disk is ejected; an afterimage remains.

SID/65 × PAULA/68 · SM003 “Wo die Maschinen sich treffen” · track 2 · 92 BPM · D minor

Fensterlicht auf Magnetband

A tape runs between two reels across the frame. At the record head Paula writes her samples as marks – the kick as bars, keys as strokes at their pitch, glass as bright lines, the snare as scratches, the shaker as dust – and what has been written drifts out to the left. The SID is the window light: the long triangle voices are broad beams, the pulse melody a narrow strip that moves with its pitch, the bass a light on the floor. “Band läuft aus”: the end of the tape passes the head.

SID/65 × PAULA/68 · SM003 “Wo die Maschinen sich treffen” · track 3 · 126 BPM · E minor

Zwischen zwei Takten

A tracker screen: seven columns – three SID voices, four Paula channels – one row per sixteenth, 64 rows per pattern, 16 patterns. Question on the left, answer on the right; the cursor stays in the middle while the score runs through it, and what is coming already shows faintly below. Behind it, copper bars flare with the kick drum. “Offene Türen”: the halves move apart. “Halbe Geschwindigkeit”: taller rows, slower scrolling.

SID/65 × PAULA/68 · SM003 “Wo die Maschinen sich treffen” · track 6 · 72 BPM · F♯ minor / A major

Was im Speicher bleibt

A dark field in which every sound leaves an ember. A note's place is its pitch: the note name gives the direction (circle of fifths), the octave the distance from the centre. The key becomes a region of the sky, and repetition makes it brighter. The SID melody draws lines from note to note like a star chart. The camera pulls back until the whole is a constellation; faintly, the afterimages of the other six films appear. The last chord remains.
